01 — Context

A family business losing ground to bigger players

Drake's Lawn & Pest Control has served five locations across the United States for years. But their website hadn't kept up. While competitors like TruGreen and Terminix invested heavily in their digital presence, Drake's was operating with a site that felt dated, loaded slowly, and gave visitors no clear reason to pick up the phone.

The brief was clear: redesign and rebuild the site in WordPress — modernise the brand, fix the navigation, and make 'Book a Free Pest Inspection' the most obvious thing on every page.

02 — The Problem

Four things that were actively losing them business

Before touching Figma, I spent time auditing the existing site against heuristics, reviewing client feedback, and mapping where the experience broke down. The problems weren't cosmetic — they were structural.

Navigation chaos

Pest control, lawn care, financing, and about us were all at the same level in the menu. Visitors couldn't quickly find what they needed — and most didn't try twice.

No lead capture system

The contact form was buried on a separate page with no CTA on the homepage to guide visitors toward it. Bookings were happening despite the site, not because of it.

Poor performance

Pingdom rating of C. Uncompressed images, no caching, inconsistent mobile layouts. The site was slow on desktop and broken on mobile.

Brand not working hard enough

The yellow, brown, and green palette was fine — but applied generically. Nothing felt premium or trustworthy. Alongside TruGreen, they looked like a different league entirely.

03 — Research & Discovery

Understanding the competitive landscape

The client gave me four reference sites: TruGreen, Terminix, Orkin, and Massey Services. Rather than just looking at them aesthetically, I broke down exactly what design patterns they used to build trust, guide users, and convert visits into bookings.

Three patterns showed up consistently across all four competitors that Drake's was missing:

Reviews above the fold

Every top competitor surfaced star ratings and customer counts in the hero section — before the user even started scrolling.

One dominant CTA

'Get a Free Quote' or 'Book an Inspection' — one action, repeated everywhere. No confusion about what to do next.

Service mega menus

Pest types organized into a structured dropdown — making the breadth of service feel like a feature, not a complexity.

Location-aware content

Branch pages and city landing pages tailored to local areas — giving the brand a national feel with local relevance.

04 — Design Decisions

The thinking behind the choices

This is the part most portfolios skip. Here's exactly why I made the decisions I made — not just what I did.

Why a mega menu for pest control?

The old navigation tried to hide the complexity — one 'Pest Control' link that led to a wall of text. But complexity is a trust signal in this industry. A mega menu showing 17 pest types tells the visitor: we know our stuff. It also reduces the number of clicks to reach a specific pest page from 3+ to 1.

Why move reviews to the top of the homepage?

Pest control is a high-trust purchase — someone is letting a stranger into their home. Research consistently shows that social proof needs to appear before the user is asked to take any action. Moving reviews above the fold reduced the trust gap before the CTA.

Why a sticky header with a phone number?

Analytics showed the phone was the preferred contact method. A rotating phone number in the sticky header meant that no matter where a visitor was on the site, the fastest path to contact was always visible.

Why city and branch landing pages?

Local SEO. Generic service pages rank poorly for local searches like 'pest control in [city].' Dedicated city pages with local content give Drake's a chance to rank in each of their service areas — turning the site into an organic lead source, not just a brochure.

05 — Wireframes

Structure before style

I wireframed all 10 unique page types before touching colour or typography. The goal was to validate the layout logic and content hierarchy independently of how it looked.

Design decision at wireframe stage: I tested two homepage layouts — one with the hero leading with imagery, one with a split hero (text + CTA left, trust signals right). The split version was chosen because it put the CTA and social proof in the same viewport without scrolling.

06 — Visual Design

Making the brand earn its place

The existing brand colours — yellow, brown, and green — were retained at the client's request. But the system around them was entirely rebuilt. A distinctive typeface, a custom icon set, and a clear typographic hierarchy gave the brand the premium feel it was missing.

09 — Reflection

What I'd do differently

If I had more time on this project, I would have pushed for user testing on the navigation structure before finalising it. The mega menu was designed based on competitor research and best practices — but real user feedback on how people search for pest services would have strengthened the decision.

I also would have liked to design a more robust analytics setup from the start — proper event tracking on the CTAs to measure conversion rate directly, not just traffic.

"Making something look premium isn't about complexity — it's about consistency. The brand colours were perfectly fine. What was missing was a clear system that applied them intentionally. Once that was in place, the gap between Drake's and TruGreen became much smaller than the client expected."

— Aman Patel, Lead Designer
